Context shapes every decision I make.

I choose technology based on what a situation actually requires—not what slides easiest into a stack. These are the principles I build by.

01
Technology

Vanilla-First Is Not Purism. It's Economics.

I choose frameworks when they solve an architectural problem, and avoid them when they add unnecessary weight. Every kilobyte I ship is weight someone else's device has to carry. In a classroom where students share devices and connections vary, that weight matters.

If I need component reusability across a large team, React makes sense. If I need a scoring interface that loads fast on a range of devices, vanilla JS is the responsible choice. The decision is always situational.

  • Measure the cost of every dependency in performance and maintenance
  • Use frameworks for team scale, not for personal convenience
  • Progressive enhancement: core functionality works without JS
02
Pedagogy

Computational Thinking Before Syntax

I start with how to think through a problem before introducing any tool. Pattern recognition, abstraction, decomposition, and algorithmic thinking come first. Then I ask: what is the simplest syntax that lets students practice those skills?

Usually, that means vanilla JS manipulating the DOM. Students see cause and effect immediately. They understand why something works before they learn the shortcuts.

  • Every lesson must have a "why" before the "how"
  • Projects should fail in instructive ways, not mysterious ways
  • Context is provided alongside syntax; thinking is required
03
Design

Respect the User's Context

A teacher scoring TESTING speaking tests between classes is a professional with a deadline, working on whatever device is at hand, in a room where the power might cut. Every design decision has to survive that reality.

This means large touch targets, offline-first data, clear visual hierarchy, and zero assumptions about screen size or connectivity. The goal is to reduce the teacher's cognitive load, not increase their time-on-app.

  • Design for the worst device and connection you can imagine
  • Every interaction should save time, not consume it
  • Accessibility is not a checklist; it's the default
04
AI

AI Is a Reasoning Partner, Not a Replacement

I use AI to speed up exploration and generate starting points, but I treat every output as a draft that needs review. My agents are trained on patterns from experts I trust, with reasoning and standards built in from the start.

The goal is to think faster, not to think less. Every AI-generated solution must be explainable in plain language before it becomes part of a project.

  • Every AI-generated solution must be explainable in plain language
  • Refactor AI output before shipping; never paste blindly
  • Use AI to augment judgment, not replace it
