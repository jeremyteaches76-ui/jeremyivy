I don't believe in best practices. I believe in context.

The same tool that saves a San Francisco startup will break a Cambodian classroom. I've spent 20 years learning which abstractions are worth their weight and which ones are just cargo culting. These are the principles I build by.

01
Technology

Vanilla-First Is Not Purism. It's Economics.

I don't avoid frameworks, but I only use them when they are best fit for a solution. , Every kilobyte I ship is weight someone else's device has to carry. In a classroom where students share devices and connections vary, that weight is real.

I use them frameworks when the problem is architectural, and avoid them when they're inelegant. If I need component reusability across a large team, React makes sense. If I need a scoring interface that loads fast on a range of devices, vanilla JS is the responsible choice.

  • Measure the cost of every dependency in performance and maintenance
  • Use frameworks for team scale, not for personal convenience
  • Progressive enhancement: core functionality works without JS
02
Pedagogy

Computational Thinking Before Syntax

Most ICT curricula teach tools: "This is Scratch. This is HTML." They don't teach thinking. Students finish these courses able to drag blocks or copy tags, but unable to decompose a problem or trace a logical chain.

I reverse-engineer from outcomes. What does a student need to think like a programmer? Pattern recognition, abstraction, decomposition, algorithmic thinking. Then I ask: what's the simplest syntax that lets them practice those skills? Usually, it's vanilla JS manipulating the DOM. They see cause and effect immediately.

  • Every lesson must have a "why" before the "how"
  • Projects should fail in instructive ways, not mysterious ways
  • Context is provided alongside syntax; thinking is required
03
Design

Respect the User's Context

A teacher scoring IELTS speaking tests between classes is not a "user." They're a professional with a deadline, working on whatever device is at hand, in a room where the power might cut. Every design decision has to survive that reality.

This means large touch targets, offline-first data, clear visual hierarchy, and zero assumptions about screen size or connectivity. The goal is to reduce the teacher's cognitive load, not increase their time-on-app.

  • Design for the worst device and connection you can imagine
  • Every interaction should save time, not consume it
  • Accessibility is not a checklist; it's the default
04
AI

AI Is a Reasoning Partner, Not a Replacement

The goal is to use AI as a worker, and not a decision maker. I use AI to enhance my productivity. I let it search and suggest solutions, to get me thinking.

I don't let it write code blindly.

My AI agents are trained on the coding and design patterns of experts and mentors I trust, and I bake reasoning and standards into my coding agents.

  • Every AI-generated solution must be explainable in plain language
  • Refactor AI output before shipping; never paste blindly
  • Teach people to interrogate AI, not defer to it
